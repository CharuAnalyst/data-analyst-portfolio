## Executive Summary

This project analyzes Superstore retail performance using an interactive Power BI dashboard. The dashboard brings together sales, profit, orders, customer segments, payment behavior, shipping modes, category performance, and regional trends into one business view.

The main purpose of this analysis is to help stakeholders understand **where revenue is coming from, whether sales are profitable, which segments drive performance, and where deeper business investigation is needed**.

## Key Insights

### Revenue Drivers
The dashboard shows that **Office Supplies and Technology-related categories contribute strongly to sales**, while sub-categories such as **Phones, Tables, and Storage** appear as important revenue contributors.

### Customer Behavior
The **Consumer segment** shows a strong share of sales in the selected view. Payment mode analysis also shows that **Cash on Delivery has a high visible share**, which may affect operations, returns, and cash flow.

### Profitability Watchpoint
Sales and profit do not always move together. Some areas with high sales may need deeper margin analysis before making business decisions.

### Regional Performance
The dashboard uses region filters to help compare business performance across markets. This makes it easier to identify where sales strength or profit weakness is concentrated.

---

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Review Profit Alongside Sales
High sales should not automatically be treated as high performance. Profit needs to be checked at the same time to avoid low-margin growth.

### 2. Investigate COD-Heavy Orders
Since COD has a high visible share, the business should review delivery success rate, return rate, and operational cost for COD orders.

### 3. Focus On High-Performing Sub-Categories
Sub-categories such as Phones, Tables, and Storage should be reviewed for pricing, demand patterns, and profitability contribution.

### 4. Use Region Filters For Market Strategy
Regional filtering can help identify which markets need stronger sales campaigns, better logistics, or deeper profitability review.

### 5. Track Monthly Profit Dips
Monthly profit dips should be reviewed separately from sales peaks because profit is the stronger business health indicator.
